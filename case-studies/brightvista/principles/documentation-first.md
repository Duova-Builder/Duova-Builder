# Principle: Documentation Is Part of the Product

At DovaBuilder, documentation is considered part of the final product - not an optional extra.

Every important decision should be documented, including the available options, the reasoning behind the final choice, and the outcome after implementation. Documentation creates a transparent record that helps both the client and future maintainers understand how and why the project evolved.

## Why this principle matters

### 1. Decisions should be traceable

Every technical and business decision has a reason. Recording those reasons prevents future confusion and eliminates unnecessary guesswork.

Examples include:

* Why a particular domain name was chosen.
* Why a specific hosting provider or pricing plan was selected.
* Why WordPress was chosen instead of another platform.
* Why certain plugins, themes, or services were adopted.

### 2. Knowledge should not disappear

Projects often continue long after launch. Without documentation, valuable knowledge exists only in conversations or in someone's memory. Good documentation preserves that knowledge and makes it available to everyone involved.

### 3. Better maintenance and future improvements

When changes are needed months or years later, documentation provides the context behind previous decisions. This makes maintenance faster, reduces mistakes, and helps future developers build on existing work instead of starting from scratch.

### 4. Transparency builds trust

Clients should understand not only what was delivered, but also why it was delivered that way. Clear documentation makes the decision-making process transparent and demonstrates that recommendations are based on thoughtful evaluation rather than personal preference.

## What we document

Whenever practical, project documentation includes:

* Project requirements.
* Available options and alternatives.
* Decision rationale.
* Configuration and deployment steps.
* Cost breakdowns.
* Verification and setup procedures.
* SEO and marketing strategy.
* Results, lessons learned, and future improvements.

## The goal

A successful project is more than a functioning website. It should also leave behind a well-organized knowledge base that explains the project, supports future maintenance, and enables continuous improvement.
